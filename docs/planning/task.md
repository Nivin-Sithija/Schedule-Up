This Week's Theme: Side Hustle
Build something with open-weight models or open-source AI that someone would actually pay for.

That can mean running an open-weight model, building on an open-source agent harness or framework, running inference locally, or all three. Whatever you pick, the open pieces should be what makes your project work.

Think small, specific, and useful. The best builds here solve a real problem for a real person, whether that's a freelancer, a small business owner, a niche hobby community, or your friend who keeps complaining about the same tedious task. A few ideas to get you going:

An invoice and receipt sorter for freelancers that runs locally, so client data never leaves their laptop

A listing writer for Etsy or eBay sellers that turns a few product photos into a polished description

A menu translator for a local restaurant that handles their specific dishes better than a generic tool

In your post, tell us why open innovation matters for what you built. Does running your own model keep costs low enough that the business actually works? Keep your customers' data private? Let you fine-tune for a niche that a general-purpose API handles poorly? Avoid getting locked into someone else's pricing? Tell us where your open-based approach worked better than a closed one.

Bonus points if you land your first user. Get it in front of someone, let them use it, and tell us how it went. Did they pay? Would they? What did they ask for next?

## Current implementation scope — 15 October 2026

ScheduleUp now combines WhatsApp and personal Telegram conversations in one account workspace. Conversations offers channel icons and All/WhatsApp/Telegram filters. Tasks share one deadline calendar and retain their source channel and supporting chat evidence. Owner-reviewed drafts, templates, approvals and completion use the existing Gemma/Mastra/Temporal/Tiger pipeline. Accounts and allowlists remain isolated; identities on different platforms are not silently merged. Telegram uses an open-source server-side MTProto client with QR login and encrypted sessions. iPhone SMS remains a future bridge, not an implemented web-worker feature.

Validation requires both automated regressions and a real local-model workflow check. Live provider checks must be recorded separately after the owner registers, links their accounts and sends the requested client messages. Never describe synthetic input or an unpaired connector as a completed live integration.
